## EXPERIMENT_GATE audit

### Intended question and paper-value admission

The gate selected exactly **RQ3: How accurate are the tags?** and tested one bounded mechanism uncertainty: whether a grouped-source scalar cutoff over the unchanged action-transition NPMI score transfers between two trajectory domains after occurrence-weighted empirical-CDF normalization.

This is not a new algorithmic abstraction, RQ, dataset, score term, visible feature, or target-specific threshold. It directly distinguishes two explanations already exposed by the existing artifacts:

1. raw NPMI cutoff values fail to transfer primarily because their numerical scales differ; or
2. OSWorld-Human and CodeTraceBench encode different desired grouping semantics, so a monotone rescaling cannot transfer one scalar decision.

The exact experiment was not mathematically redundant with Step 0030's per-domain grouped calibration. It was nevertheless incremental at the whole-paper level because both target populations and the recurrence family had already been used extensively. The plan correctly classified a possible positive as supporting reused-population mechanism/generalization evidence rather than untouched confirmation. This limited paper value does not invalidate admission: it followed the user's then-active request to improve the existing algorithm on already-run trajectories, used a single simple candidate, and had a concrete adoption decision. It does mean this branch earns no further normalization or cutoff attempt after the present result.

### Literature and real-evidence contact

The bounded source screen used primary AAAI, ACL, and EMNLP sources to establish that source-to-target threshold transfer, limited-label calibration, and rank-scale comparison are recognized protocol families. It explicitly refused a novelty claim for empirical CDFs, quantiles, or calibration. Real evidence came from the complete registered OSWorld-Human and CodeTraceBench artifacts, not from same-context reflection, synthetic toy data, or a checker result.

The source screen was appropriately scoped to experiment admission. ### Plan and plan review

The first plan review found a real blocker: the plan scheduled 10,000 paired bootstrap draws per target but initially did not use uncertainty in the support rule. The repaired plan, approved before real preflight, made the outcome classes mutually exclusive and required interval support for a positive result. No target result, new workload, or extra mechanism was inspected to make that repair.

The final plan used:

- current label-free recurrence as the operational main baseline;
- direct raw-cutoff transfer as the equal-information normalization comparison;
- existing per-domain grouped calibration only as a higher-information upper-bound control;
- operation-weighted B-cubed F1 as the primary partition metric; and
- exact boundary precision, recall, and F1 as standard secondary transition metrics.

These metrics are established and claim-matched. B-cubed measures operation-partition agreement, while exact boundary P/R/F1 measures adjacent transition decisions. Their different rankings describe merge/fragmentation tradeoffs; they do not make the experiment or its negative result metrically invalid.

### Preflight and complete run

The real preflight exercised both source-fit and target-prediction paths, persisted real predictions, and loaded scoring information afterward where the implementation contract allowed. It correctly returned dependency-only status and was not interpreted scientifically.

The unchanged full implementation completed every registered cell:

| Target | Sessions | Operations | Adjacent pairs | Bootstrap draws |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| OSWorld-Human | 287 | 3,978 | 3,691 | 10,000 |
| CodeTraceBench failed target | 405 | 20,866 | 20,461 | 10,000 |

Every target operation received one oracle and one assignment per method; every adjacent pair received one decision per method. The label-free baseline reproduced Step 0024 exactly. Candidate and raw-transfer conditions used the same source annotations, target-visible actions, target reference distribution, scalar-decision budget, and target rows.

The complete primary results are:

| Target | Percentile transfer B-cubed F1 | Label-free B-cubed F1 | Candidate minus label-free, paired 95% interval |
|---|---:|---:|---:|

Percentile transfer beats direct raw-cutoff transfer by +0.037077 on OSWorld and +0.074719 on CodeTrace, with wholly positive paired intervals. Thus the transform corrects part of the raw score-scale mismatch, but the registered cross-domain claim fails decisively against the current label-free constructor in both directions. The candidate over-merges OSWorld into 1,316 groups and over-fragments CodeTrace into 12,941 groups.

### Raw evidence and independent result review

The result review independently parsed 68,996 raw records, reconstructed all 24,152 unscored predictions from source-visible operations, reimplemented NPMI/CDF/cutoff fitting and both standard metric families, replayed all stored bootstrap draws, and generated a second independent stratified bootstrap. It reproduced all populations, decisions, point estimates, and interval signs.

Two deviations are real but non-invalidating:

1. the OSWorld loader parses label-bearing records to enforce the already-fixed exact-aligned, multi-operation eligibility population before returning actions, although no group identity or boundary enters prediction; and
2. the cutoff fitter represents a separating percentile with an empty-interval midpoint, but no target percentile falls in the affected interval, so every target decision is unchanged.

These qualifications must remain in provenance. They do not convert the negative into leakage, an invalid run, or metric ambiguity. The correct result classification is:

- **run status:** valid and complete;
- **tested hypothesis:** contradicted;
- **research value:** supporting mechanism boundary;
- **paper impact:** no positive reader-facing result and no product-path adoption.

### Experiment-gate conclusion

The gate solved its declared narrow question completely. Percentile normalization is better than raw scalar transfer but worse than current label-free recurrence on both registered domains. The `RQ3 -> cross-domain scalar percentile transfer` branch is closed. No second normalization, cutoff, score term, target-specific percentile, Rust port, or relabeled retry is justified.

This closure does not answer all of RQ3 and does not weaken its hypothesis. It also does not change the thesis, the operation-stack model, or the submodule story.
