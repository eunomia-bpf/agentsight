## Blind Full-Paper Assessment

Before reading Step 0030's internal record, the paper presented one coherent
position:

> AI-agent teams accumulate many executions, while existing traces primarily
> preserve individual execution occurrences. Profiling should instead
> attribute additive activity and effects to reusable responsibility categories
> across those executions. AgentProf realizes this with uniform operations and
> query-time operation stacks.

The exact memorable principle is visible in the abstract, Introduction, and
Conclusion: **Agent observability needs profiling, not only debugging.** The
challenged belief is not the strawman that traces or execution trees are
useless; it is that an emitted per-run execution structure is sufficient as the
only responsibility hierarchy for recurring cross-run cost, failure, safety,
and wasted-work questions. The artifact/mechanism chain is also reconstructable:

```text
heterogeneous agent activity and system effects
-> uniform weighted operations
-> declared, derived, or induced responsibility fields
-> query-time operation stacks
-> conserved folded profiles and selectable weight views
```

The Evaluation explicitly states exactly four paper-level RQs:

1. resource attribution;
2. correspondence to real problems;
3. tag accuracy; and
4. profiling cost.

The strongest blind-read reject argument remains evidence completeness rather
than Step 0030's local mechanism: RQ3 asks broadly about task, phase, action,
and boundary identity, while the current positive evidence establishes task
partitions and operation partitions/boundaries but explicitly leaves phase,
action, and literal tag-name accuracy outside the current evidence. This makes
the complete paper incomplete-but-promising rather than submission-ready; it
does not invalidate a scoped additional RQ3 result.

The paper's research taste remains directionally strong. The problem is real
and recurring, the principle is simple and separable from the artifact name,
and the design uses only two core abstractions. The paper avoids turning
calibration, NPMI, B-cubed, controls, or profile export into additional named
contributions. The largest scientific risk is that reviewers may view the
algorithmic pieces as known components unless the complete evaluation makes the
agent-profiling principle and its decision value—not the scalar cutoff—the
substantive contribution.
